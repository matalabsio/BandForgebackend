The increasing use of online education has sparked discussion about whether traditional classrooms will become obsolete. Proponents argue that digital platforms offer flexibility, lower costs, and access for learners who cannot attend campus regularly. Recorded lectures and interactive tools can also allow students to revisit difficult material at their own pace.

Critics counter that face-to-face learning develops social skills, immediate feedback, and motivation that screens cannot fully replace. Laboratory work, group projects, and informal discussion often work better in person. In my opinion, online education will continue to expand, but it is unlikely to eliminate classrooms entirely. A blended model — combining digital materials with in-person seminars — offers the most practical path forward for most institutions.
