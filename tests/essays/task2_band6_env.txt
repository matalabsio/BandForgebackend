Environmental problems such as climate change and plastic pollution require urgent action, yet people disagree about who should lead the response. Some argue individuals must change their habits, while others insist only governments and corporations can make a real difference. I believe both levels of action are essential.

At the individual level, reducing waste, using public transport, and choosing reusable products can cut emissions and set social norms. When millions of people make small changes, markets respond by offering greener products. Personal responsibility also builds political support for stronger policies.

However, individuals cannot redesign energy systems or regulate international shipping alone. Governments can set emission targets, invest in renewables, and ban single-use plastics. Large companies control supply chains and packaging, so corporate regulation is equally important.

Overall, blaming only one side is unhelpful. Sustainable progress depends on citizens choosing better habits while governments and businesses create the rules and infrastructure that make those habits practical.
